### **Scrum Software Development Methodology**

Scrum, is a lightweight project management framework that uses an iterative and incremental approach.

**Scrum** is an agile framework for software development that emphasizes iterative, incremental progress, collaboration, flexibility, and transparency. It is particularly useful for managing complex projects and delivering high-quality software through regular feedback loops and teamwork. The Scrum methodology is built around the concept of **sprints**, which are short, time-boxed development cycles (usually 2-4 weeks).


In Scrum, the product owner – a person with the power to decide what items will go into the application – plays a central role. This person needs to play an active role throughout the development process.

The product owner works closely with the development team to create a prioritized list of system functionality, called the product backlog. The product backlog consists of whatever needs to be done to deliver a working software system successfully. The items in the backlog can be things such as the features of the application, bugs that need to be fixed, and non-functional requirements such as certification, accessibility, and data integrity. When the properties in the backlog have been prioritized, a team of developers (and potentially other roles, if needed) will start to develop in what can be called potentially shippable increments. This means that the team will take some of the highest prioritized items from the backlog and start to implement them during a short time frame, known as a sprint. A sprint will typically last for somewhere between 14 and 30 days. The outcome of the sprint is preferably fully functional so that it can immediately be put into production, and users can start using this functionality. The team will then start over with a new sprint. This will be repeated as many times as necessary.

### **1. Scrum Roles**

Scrum defines three main roles, each with its own responsibilities:

#### a. **Product Owner**
   - **Responsibilities**:
     - Owns the product vision and ensures the team is working on the highest-priority items that will deliver value to the customer.
     - Maintains and prioritizes the **Product Backlog** (a list of features, bugs, enhancements, etc.).
     - Represents the voice of the customer and stakeholders.
     - Makes decisions on feature requirements and scope.
     - Clarifies requirements and resolves any ambiguities in user stories.
   
#### b. **Scrum Master**
   - **Responsibilities**:
     - Acts as a facilitator for the Scrum team and ensures that the Scrum process is being followed.
     - Removes any **impediments** that may block the team's progress (e.g., technical issues, communication barriers).
     - Protects the team from external distractions and ensures a focused work environment.
     - Coaches the team on Agile principles and best practices.
     - Ensures Scrum events (meetings) happen as scheduled and are productive.
   
#### c. **Development Team**
   - **Responsibilities**:
     - Self-organizing and cross-functional group responsible for delivering potentially shippable increments of the product.
     - Developers, designers, testers, and other specialists collaborate to build and test the product.
     - The development team is empowered to make decisions on how to complete tasks and solve problems.
     - Members of the team should have all the skills necessary to deliver the product increment.

---

### **2. Scrum Artifacts**

Scrum defines three key **artifacts** that ensure transparency, clarity, and continuous progress.

#### a. **Product Backlog**
   - A prioritized list of **features**, **requirements**, **bug fixes**, **technical work**, and **enhancements** for the product.
   - Maintained and prioritized by the Product Owner.
   - The Product Backlog is **dynamic**, meaning it evolves and changes as new requirements emerge or existing ones are refined.
   - Each item in the backlog is often described as a **User Story** or **Product Backlog Item (PBI)**.

#### b. **Sprint Backlog**
   - A subset of the Product Backlog that the team commits to completing during a particular Sprint.
   - It is a list of **tasks** that the team has identified and selected for the current sprint, broken down from the backlog items.
   - The Sprint Backlog evolves during the sprint, with tasks added or removed based on progress and new learnings.

#### c. **Increment**
   - The **increment** represents the working product that is delivered at the end of each sprint.
   - It includes all the features or functionalities that were completed during the sprint, and it must meet the **Definition of Done (DoD)**, ensuring it is potentially shippable and of high quality.
   - The Increment must be usable and add value to the product or project.

---

### **3. Scrum Events**

Scrum defines five **key events** that structure the work and ensure regular reflection, adaptation, and planning.

#### a. **Sprint**
   - The **Sprint** is the core event in Scrum—a **time-boxed iteration** during which a potentially shippable product increment is created.
   - Sprints are typically 2-4 weeks long, with a consistent duration throughout the project.
   - Each sprint has a clear goal, defined scope, and ends with a **Sprint Review** and **Sprint Retrospective**.

#### b. **Sprint Planning**
   - The **Sprint Planning** meeting kicks off each sprint and is attended by the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Development Team.
   - **Purpose**: Determine what work will be done during the sprint.
     - The **Product Owner** presents the highest-priority items from the **Product Backlog**.
     - The **Development Team** selects the items they can commit to completing during the sprint and creates a **Sprint Backlog**.
     - The team collaboratively defines the **Sprint Goal**, a clear, concise statement of what will be achieved during the sprint.

#### c. **Daily Scrum (Daily Standup)**
   - A **15-minute time-boxed meeting** held every day during the sprint.
   - The Development Team answers three questions:
     1. **What did I accomplish yesterday?**
     2. **What will I work on today?**
     3. **Are there any obstacles or impediments?**
   - The Scrum Master helps ensure that this event stays focused and removes any blockers.
   - It’s a short, focused meeting to ensure team alignment and adjust work plans for the day.

#### d. **Sprint Review**
   - At the end of each sprint, the team holds a **Sprint Review** to present the completed work.
   - **Purpose**: Demonstrate the Increment (working software) to the stakeholders and gather feedback.
     - The Product Owner ensures that the **Product Backlog** is up to date based on the sprint’s progress and any new feedback.
     - Stakeholders and the team discuss what went well, what didn’t, and what improvements can be made.
   
#### e. **Sprint Retrospective**
   - The **Sprint Retrospective** occurs after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint Planning session.
   - **Purpose**: Reflect on the sprint process and identify ways to improve.
     - The team discusses what went well, what challenges were encountered, and what could be improved in the next sprint.
     - The goal is to improve the Scrum process, identify bottlenecks, and continuously optimize the way the team works.

---

### **4. Scrum Artifacts and Metrics**

In addition to the core artifacts, Scrum teams often use a variety of **metrics** to track progress, including:

#### a. **Burndown Chart**
   - A graphical representation showing the amount of work remaining in the sprint (or project) over time.
   - The chart helps the team track progress and make adjustments if necessary.

#### b. **Velocity**
   - **Velocity** is a metric that tracks how much work the team completes in each sprint.
   - This helps the team gauge their capacity and make better estimates for future sprints.

---

### **5. Key Principles of Scrum**

1. **Empirical Process Control**: Scrum is based on the idea that knowledge comes from experience and making decisions based on what is known.
   - The three pillars of empirical process control are:
     - **Transparency**: All aspects of the process must be visible to those responsible for the outcome.
     - **Inspection**: Scrum artifacts and progress toward the goals are regularly inspected to detect potential problems.
     - **Adaptation**: Adjustments are made as needed to optimize the process and product.

2. **Self-Organizing Teams**: Scrum teams are encouraged to organize themselves, solve problems autonomously, and take responsibility for the work they commit to.

3. **Collaboration**: Active collaboration between the Scrum team and stakeholders is essential for success.

4. **Focus**: Scrum encourages teams to focus on delivering value during each sprint, ensuring that they don’t waste effort on unnecessary features.

---

### **Conclusion**

Scrum is a lightweight, flexible framework that provides structure for managing software development through **iterative cycles** (sprints) and frequent, transparent feedback. It empowers teams to improve continuously, deliver working software regularly, and respond to changing requirements efficiently. The roles, events, and artifacts in Scrum help create a dynamic, collaborative environment where teams can focus on high-priority tasks, deliver value, and continuously improve their processes.



















Scrum introduced and formalized several **specific practices** that distinguish it from other Agile and traditional methodologies. These practices are designed to promote **empirical process control, collaboration, and iterative delivery**. Here are the **key practices that Scrum formalized** that set it apart:  

---

## **1️⃣ Scrum Roles (Unique Team Structure)**
Scrum **redefines team roles** to optimize collaboration and accountability. Unlike traditional project management, which has **project managers**, Scrum relies on **self-organizing teams** with the following key roles:  

| **Scrum Role** | **Description** | **How It’s Different** |
|--------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Scrum Master** | Facilitates Scrum processes, removes obstacles, and ensures Agile best practices are followed. | Replaces traditional **project managers** with a **servant-leadership role** that coaches rather than commands. |
| **Product Owner** | Defines the product vision, manages the backlog, and prioritizes user stories. | Unlike traditional business analysts, the **Product Owner has authority over priorities and business value**. |
| **Development Team** | A cross-functional group that designs, develops, and tests the product in iterations. | Self-organizing, with **no hierarchy**, unlike traditional **development teams that follow top-down direction**. |

---

## **2️⃣ Time-Boxed Iterations (Sprints)**
- **Scrum formalized fixed-length iterations** called **Sprints**, usually **1-4 weeks** long.  
- Each Sprint delivers a **potentially shippable product increment**.  
- Unlike **Kanban (continuous flow)** or **Waterfall (long project phases)**, Scrum uses **strictly time-boxed iterations** to enforce rapid delivery.  

---

## **3️⃣ Sprint Planning (Structured Planning)**
- A **dedicated event** at the start of each Sprint where the team commits to work.  
- The team **pulls prioritized items** from the **Product Backlog** into the **Sprint Backlog**.  
- Unlike **Extreme Programming (XP), which allows flexible mid-iteration changes**, Scrum **locks Sprint scope after planning**.  

---

## **4️⃣ Daily Scrum (Daily Stand-Up)**
- A **15-minute daily meeting** where the team discusses:  
  1. **What they did yesterday**  
  2. **What they will do today**  
  3. **Any blockers**  
- Unlike **traditional daily status meetings**, the Scrum Master **does NOT lead**—the team self-organizes.  
- Unlike **Kanban, which focuses on continuous flow**, Scrum ensures daily alignment through this structured ritual.  

---

## **5️⃣ Sprint Review (Demo & Feedback)**
- Held at the **end of each Sprint** to demonstrate the **completed work** to stakeholders.  
- Focuses on **user feedback and business value**, unlike traditional methodologies that delay feedback until after full development.  
- Unlike **Waterfall**, where reviews happen at the project end, Scrum ensures **frequent feedback loops**.  

---

## **6️⃣ Sprint Retrospective (Continuous Improvement)**
- A **dedicated improvement session** after each Sprint where the team reflects on:  
  - **What went well?**  
  - **What didn’t?**  
  - **How to improve next Sprint?**  
- Unlike **XP (which focuses mainly on technical improvements)**, Scrum retrospectives cover **team dynamics, processes, and collaboration**.  

---

## **7️⃣ Product Backlog (Evolving Prioritized Work Queue)**
- A **living document** of all product requirements, maintained by the **Product Owner**.  
- **Reprioritized continuously** based on business needs.  
- Unlike **Waterfall (fixed requirements at the start)**, Scrum allows backlog changes **before every Sprint**.  
- Unlike **Kanban (which lacks structured backlog management)**, Scrum uses clear backlog **refinement sessions**.  

---

## **8️⃣ Definition of Done (DoD)**
- A **formalized team agreement** on when a backlog item is considered “done.”  
- Ensures quality control and prevents incomplete work from being released.  
- Unlike XP, which focuses on **unit testing and code completion**, Scrum requires a **potentially shippable state** (fully tested, integrated, and documented).  

---

## **9️⃣ Self-Organizing, Cross-Functional Teams**
- Teams **decide how to do the work**—no **manager assigning tasks**.  
- Unlike **Waterfall, where developers and testers are separate**, Scrum teams are **cross-functional** (developers, testers, designers work together).  
- Unlike **SAFe (which enforces hierarchical Agile roles)**, Scrum keeps **teams flat and autonomous**.  

---

## **🔟 No Mid-Sprint Changes (Sprint Commitment)**
- Once a Sprint starts, **the work is locked**—no changes are introduced.  
- This prevents **scope creep**, unlike **Kanban, which allows continuous backlog adjustments**.  
- Unlike **Extreme Programming (XP), where customers can request changes mid-iteration**, Scrum enforces **Sprint focus**.  

---

## **Comparison: Scrum vs Other Agile Methodologies**
| **Practice** | **Scrum** | **Kanban** | **XP (Extreme Programming)** | **SAFe (Scaled Agile)** |
|-------------|---------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **Iterations** | **Sprints (1-4 weeks)** | Continuous flow | Iterations (1-2 weeks) | Program Increments (8-12 weeks) |
| **Work in Progress (WIP) Limits** | No strict WIP limits | **Yes** (Explicit WIP limits per column) | No WIP limits | No strict WIP limits |
| **Role of Scrum Master** | **Yes** (Facilitator, not manager) | No specific role | No specific role | Yes (Scaled version of Scrum Master) |
| **Mid-Iteration Changes** | ❌ **Not allowed** | ✅ Allowed | ✅ Allowed | ❌ Not allowed |
| **Daily Meeting** | **Yes** (Daily Scrum) | Optional | **Yes** (Stand-up) | **Yes** (Scrum of Scrums) |
| **Engineering Practices** | Not explicitly defined | Not explicitly defined | **Yes** (TDD, Pair Programming, CI/CD) | Not explicitly defined |
| **Planning** | **Sprint Planning** | Continuous | Continuous | **Program Increment Planning** |

---

## **Conclusion**
Scrum **formalized a set of structured Agile practices** that ensure **predictability, collaboration, and continuous improvement**. While it shares similarities with other Agile methods, its **time-boxed iterations, self-organizing roles, commitment to Sprint goals, and structured ceremonies** set it apart.  




































 Scrum Phases

Scrum is an Agile framework that defines a set structure for iterations called Sprints. These phases occur within each sprint (usually lasting 2-4 weeks):
a. Sprint Planning

    Objective: Define the goal for the sprint and identify the user stories or tasks to be completed.
    Key Activities:
        Product Owner presents the Product Backlog.
        Development team commits to completing specific tasks from the backlog during the sprint.

b. Sprint Execution

    Objective: Develop the features, complete the tasks, and follow the sprint plan.
    Key Activities:
        Daily Scrum (or Daily Stand-up) meetings to track progress.
        Developers work collaboratively to complete user stories.
        Testing is integrated into the development process.

c. Sprint Review

    Objective: Demonstrate the working features or product increments to stakeholders.
    Key Activities:
        Show the completed work to stakeholders.
        Gather feedback for future work and determine what to prioritize next.

d. Sprint Retrospective

    Objective: Reflect on the sprint to identify areas of improvement.
    Key Activities:
        The team discusses what went well, what could be improved, and what actions should be taken for future sprints.
